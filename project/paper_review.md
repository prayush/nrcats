# Review of "Surrogate-Mediated Cross-Catalog Validation of Numerical Relativity Binary Black Hole Waveforms"

## 1. Summary of the Paper Draft

The draft presents a systematic framework for cross-catalog validation of binary black hole (BBH) waveforms generated by independent numerical relativity (NR) codes (specifically the SXS, RIT, and MAYA catalogs). Historically, direct comparisons have been hindered by **parameter-space mismatches**, as different codes use distinct initial data formalisms and gauge conditions, resulting in misaligned binary parameters.

To resolve this, the authors use the **NRSur7dq4 surrogate model** (trained exclusively on SXS quasi-circular simulations) as a common reference. By generating surrogate waveforms at the exact parameters of any given NR simulation, they eliminate the parameter mismatch. The agreement is evaluated using two metrics:
1. **Noise-weighted match** (using Advanced LIGO design sensitivity) maximized over time, phase, and rigid SO(3) rotations.
2. **Phase difference per gravitational-wave cycle**, which tracks secular phase drift.

**Key Findings:**
- Applied to a batch of 1,648 non-spinning and aligned-spin BBH simulations, the framework shows excellent baseline agreement for the dominant $(2,2)$ mode in quasi-circular configurations (mismatches routinely $< 10^{-2}$).
- By varying initial eccentricity (acting as a negative control since the surrogate assumes quasi-circular orbits), the mismatch degrades systematically following a power-law, with sub-dominant modes (e.g., $(3,3)$ and $(4,4)$) degrading more rapidly than the $(2,2)$ mode.
- Isolating the SXS dataset into "in-sample" (calibration) and "out-of-sample" simulations highlights the interpolation boundaries of the surrogate model. RIT and MAYA catalogs show slightly wider match distributions, particularly near the edges of the surrogate's training domain (e.g., $q \sim 4$, high effective spin).
- Future extensions outlined include BMS supertranslation corrections and full analysis of precessing-spin systems.

## 2. Suggestions for Additions to the Paper

While the draft is very comprehensive, incorporating the following points could strengthen its impact:

> [!TIP]
> **Expand on Figure 4:** The text mentions Figure 4 (a heatmap in $(q, \chi_{\rm eff})$ parameter space) but does not discuss its specific findings in the main text. Adding a paragraph in Section IV.B.2 detailing where the "hotspots" of mismatch occur in the parameter space would be very insightful.

- **Impact on Parameter Estimation (PE):** While a mismatch of $10^{-2}$ is deemed "excellent" for Advanced LIGO, it may not be sufficient for next-generation detectors like Cosmic Explorer, Einstein Telescope, or LISA. Briefly contextualizing what mismatch thresholds are required for different SNR regimes would make the results more actionable.
- **Comparison with Other Models:** Since `NRSur7dq4` is trained strictly on SXS waveforms, the pipeline effectively measures "agreement with SXS." It could be valuable to mention (perhaps in the Discussion) whether using a phenomenological model (like `IMRPhenomXPHM` or `SEOBNRv4PHM`) as the mediator yields similar relative distributions between RIT and MAYA.
- **Detailed Limitations of the Surrogate:** The phase errors for the $(4,4)$ mode are high (Section IV.A). It would be useful to explicitly state if this is due to a lack of $\ell=4$ training data or intrinsic difficulties in modeling higher harmonics.
- **Computational Cost:** Mention the computational expense of this pipeline, particularly the differential evolution global optimizer used for the SO(3) frame rotation optimization (Section V).
- **Data/Code Availability:** Conclude with a statement on whether the full tabulated match results and the comparison pipeline scripts will be made open-source (e.g., via Zenodo or GitHub) to allow the community to reproduce and build upon this work.

## 3. Research Questions of Interest

Framing the narrative around the following questions will help attract both the observational and theoretical communities.

### For Gravitational-Wave Astronomers
1. **Are current NR waveforms accurate enough for next-generation detectors?**
   Does the inter-catalog mismatch floor (e.g., $10^{-3}$) dominate over statistical errors for high-SNR events expected in O5 or Cosmic Explorer?
2. **How much do sub-dominant modes differ across catalogs, and will this bias tests of General Relativity (GR)?**
   If different codes predict differing $(3,3)$ or $(4,4)$ amplitudes, how does this propagate into multipolar consistency tests of GR?
3. **At what eccentricity do quasi-circular waveform models fail completely?**
   The power-law degradation shown in the paper answers this, but framing it around PE bias for eccentric dynamical captures (e.g., in globular clusters) is highly relevant.
4. **Is the parameter-space boundary of our models safe?**
   How rapidly do waveforms diverge at $q=4$ or high spins, and how cautious should we be when analyzing highly asymmetric events?

### For Numerical Relativists
1. **Where do independent NR codes disagree the most?**
   Are discrepancies dominated by high spins, high mass ratios, or specific higher-order modes like $(4,4)$?
2. **How significant is the BMS supertranslation ambiguity in practice?**
   Does aligning BMS frames fix the remaining mismatches, or is the residual error dominated by genuine numerical truncation?
3. **Is the interpolation error of surrogate models larger than inter-code physical differences?**
   The comparison of in-sample vs. out-of-sample SXS waveforms provides a crucial benchmark for evaluating the data-driven limitations of surrogate modeling.
4. **How do initial data formulations manifest in the long-term phase accumulation?**
   Can the differences between SpEC's XCTS approach and the moving-punctures/Bowen-York approach be traced to specific phase drifts during the inspiral?
5. **How accurate is the extraction of higher harmonics?**
   Given the differing distributions for $(3,3)$ and $(4,4)$ matches across SXS, RIT, and MAYA, are some extraction techniques (e.g., RWZ vs. $\Psi_4$) systematically better for higher-order modes?
